What does “Neutralised” mean?

Definitions, what counts as a yellow / red flag, and how laps are flagged

Everything on this page counts race laps only: no practice, qualifying or formation laps.

Neutralised lap

A race lap where the whole field was slowed and no racing was possible: behind the Safety Car, under a Full Course Yellow, or a lap interrupted by a red flag. These are the “wasted” laps: they count towards race distance but nobody is racing.

Green lap

Every other race lap: the field is racing at full speed, even if there was a waved yellow somewhere on the circuit.

Clean race

A race with zero neutralised laps from start to flag.

Red-flag minutes

Time the race was stopped, when it shows up inside a timed lap. The lap itself is also counted as neutralised.

What counts and what doesn't

SituationNeutralised?Why
Safety Car periodYes The whole field queues behind the Safety Car. These laps run roughly 40–100% slower than racing pace, so they are always caught.
Full Course Yellow (FCY) / Virtual Safety CarYes A speed limit applies across the whole track, so every car slows. Counted when it slows the leaders by more than on that lap. A very short FCY that clips one lap only slightly can fall under that.
Red flag (race stopped)Yes The lap the race is stopped on, plus any Safety Car laps after the restart. If the stoppage happens inside a timed lap (lap more than normal pace), its length is also shown in red-flag minutes.
Safety Car start (lap 1 behind the SC)Yes Same as any Safety Car lap.
Local / waved yellow at one marshal postNo No overtaking in one sector only. The rest of the lap is raced and the leaders lose a second or two, far under the threshold. This is not what the tracker is measuring.
Normal standing-start lap 1No Lap 1 is a bit slower (about 5–15%) because cars start from rest, but it is raced.
One driver spins, crashes or pitsNo Only the top 3 finishers' lap times are used and the middle one is taken, so one car's problem can't flag a lap. It only counts if the incident brings out a Safety Car or FCY.
Formation lapNo Not a race lap, so it is not in the timing data.

How a lap gets flagged

  1. Lead pace: for every lap, take the lap times of the top 3 finishers and use the middle one.
  2. Normal pace: the race's typical full-speed lap (the median of laps within 10% of the quickest lead-pace lap).
  3. Neutralised if lead pace is more than normal pace ( slower). Red flag if it is more than normal pace.
